Abstract
ClaimWise SG is a cloud-based service that helps Singapore motorists compare an own-damage insurance claim with paying
for repairs themselves after an accident has been reported. A deterministic rule engine combines policy excess, No-Claim
Discount (NCD), claim status and explicit renewal-premium scenarios to estimate multi-year costs and a break-even repair
threshold. Users may also upload current or historical insurance documents; extracted fields are confirmed by the user and
stored as a personal policy history. The prototype is deployed on AWS and will be evaluated for correctness, scalability,
document processing, usability and cost. Future premiums are treated as scenarios rather than insurer quotations.

1 Motivation & Business Case
1.1 Problem and Market Context
At the end of 2025, Singapore had 659,889 cars and station-wagons, including 524,312 private cars [4]. This provides the
market context for a service aimed at motorists dealing with post-accident insurance decisions.
Under the Motor Claims Framework (MCF), motorists must report an accident to their insurer within 24 hours or by
the next working day, including minor accidents and private settlements [1]. GIA notes that failure to comply with the
reporting condition can affect claim liability, NCD and policy renewal. However, reporting an accident and making an
own-damage claim are separate decisions. After reporting, the motorist may still need to decide whether to claim for
damage to their own vehicle or pay for the repair directly.
That decision is not determined by the repair bill alone. The main financial factors are:
• Policy excess: the policyholder may still pay part of the repair cost when making an own-damage claim.
• NCD and NCD protection: a claim may reduce the No-Claim Discount (NCD), while protection features can change
the outcome under insurer-specific terms [2].
• Future renewal premium: NCD is only one part of renewal pricing, and insurers do not publish a universal formula
for the future premium after a claim.
• Liability and third-party claims: paying for one’s own repair does not prevent another party from making a claim
arising from the same accident, so self-pay does not automatically preserve a no-claim premium path [3].
ClaimWise brings these factors into one scenario-based comparison so that the user can see both the immediate repair cost
and the possible multi-year insurance effect.
1.2 Target Users and Value Proposition
The MVP targets private motorists. A user can enter policy details manually or upload a policy schedule, renewal notice
or quotation. ClaimWise then provides:
• a claim-versus-self-pay cost comparison;
• a dynamic break-even repair threshold and sensitivity range; and
• a confirmed personal insurance history from uploaded documents.
MoneySmart and SingSaver mainly support insurance comparison, quotation and purchase [5, 6]. ClaimWise instead
focuses on the post-incident decision and combines repair cost, excess, NCD rules, policy conditions and renewal uncertainty
in one workflow.
1.3 MVP Scope
The MVP includes the rule engine, web interface, policy-document extraction and personal policy history. It does not
determine legal liability or reproduce an insurer’s proprietary renewal-pricing model. Cross-user benchmarking and
personalised premium prediction are later extensions once enough representative, consented data are available.

2 Business Model & Cloud Hosting Justification
2.1 Monetization Strategy
ClaimWise uses a B2C-first freemium model with two revenue streams:
• Advertising revenue. The calculator, document-input workflow and basic personal history remain free. Clearly labelled
advertisements may be shown for relevant automotive services, but advertising does not affect the financial result.
• Paid advanced reports. As the historical dataset grows, users may purchase one-off reports with deeper peer
comparisons, premium trends, renewal analysis and more detailed multi-year scenario analysis.
This keeps the core decision tool accessible while creating a path from broad usage to paid analysis.
2.2 B2C Data Flywheel
The data strategy does not depend on users returning a year later to report a new renewal. Instead, users can upload policy
schedules, renewal notices and quotations they already possess. ClaimWise converts these files into structured records and
immediately returns a personal history view.
With separate consent, anonymised confirmed records can later support aggregate benchmarks. As more records are
collected, these benchmarks can support more useful personalised analysis and future paid reports. Initially, simple
empirical distributions and similarity-based comparisons are sufficient; machine learning is not required for the first version.
The intended long-term relationship is:
Free Utility → More Users → More Confirmed Records → Better Benchmarks → More Valuable Reports.
2.3 Cloud Hosting and Cost Comparison
AWS provides a web-accessible deployment without the team operating physical servers. ECS Service Auto Scaling can
change application task counts with demand [7]. Document extraction also creates a workload that is heavier than the rule
calculation and can be measured separately.
The final report will compare AWS with an equivalent on-premise deployment under the same workload, availability and
backup assumptions. Costs will include measured cloud usage, annualised hardware, networking, power, backup and
operational effort.

3.3 Document Input and Policy Records
Users upload a PDF or image of an insurance document. ClaimWise extracts candidate policy fields, which the user reviews
and corrects before the information is stored as a confirmed policy record.
Each confirmed record contains the insurer, policy period, annual premium, NCD, excess, available coverage information
and document type. Purchased policies, renewal offers and comparison quotations remain distinct, so an unaccepted quote
is not treated as a paid premium.
A simplified confirmed snapshot is

timeline H1 → H2 → · · · → HT . The MVP uses this for history and descriptive analysis; later, a larger dataset could support
empirical benchmark ranges and future-premium models such as P(Pt+1 | Ht ).

5 Conclusion & Future Work
ClaimWise combines a claim-versus-self-pay calculator with policy-document extraction and a personal insurance history.
The MVP will implement the rule engine, document workflow and AWS deployment, then measure system performance,
scaling behaviour, usability and cost. With enough consented historical records, the same data can later support empirical
benchmarks and paid advanced reports. NCD and policy rules remain explicit in the rule engine, while data-driven models
are reserved for future premium estimation.
